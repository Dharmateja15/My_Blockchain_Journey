## Key Concepts Learned

### What is Blockchain?

A blockchain is a decentralized and distributed digital ledger that records transactions in blocks linked together in chronological order.

Key characteristics:

* Decentralized (no central authority)
* Transparent
* Immutable (difficult to alter past records)
* Secure through cryptography

---

### Why Was Blockchain Created?

Traditional systems rely on centralized authorities such as:

* Banks
* Governments
* Companies

Blockchain was created to enable trustless transactions, where users can interact directly without depending on a central intermediary.

---

### Bitcoin and the Double-Spending Problem

Bitcoin was introduced by Satoshi Nakamoto in 2008.

One of Bitcoin's major achievements was solving the double-spending problem.

#### What is Double Spending?

In digital systems, a user could theoretically copy digital money and spend it multiple times.

Bitcoin prevents this by:

* Maintaining a public ledger
* Verifying transactions through network consensus
* Ensuring ownership can only be transferred once

#### Bitcoin Supply

* Maximum supply: 21 million BTC
* Designed to be scarce
* Helps protect against uncontrolled inflation

---

### Ethereum and Smart Contracts

Ethereum expanded blockchain technology beyond digital currency.

It introduced Smart Contracts.

#### What is a Smart Contract?

A smart contract is a program stored on the blockchain that automatically executes when predefined conditions are met.

Benefits:

* Automation
* Transparency
* Reduced reliance on intermediaries
* Global accessibility

Example:

If Person A sends payment, ownership of a digital asset can automatically transfer to Person B.

### Stablecoins vs Bitcoin

#### Stablecoins

Example: USDC

* Designed to maintain stable value
* Usually backed by reserves such as USD
* Often targeted at a 1:1 value ratio

#### Bitcoin

* Not backed by fiat currency
* Value determined by market demand and supply
* Price fluctuates continuously

---

### Blockchain Security

Blockchain security comes from:

#### Hashing

Each block contains:

* Transaction data
* Its own hash
* The hash of the previous block

This creates a chain of interconnected blocks.

#### Immutability

Changing a past block changes its hash.

As a result:

* The chain becomes invalid
* Network participants can detect tampering

#### Consensus

The network agrees on a single valid version of the blockchain through consensus mechanisms such as:

* Proof of Work (PoW)
* Proof of Stake (PoS)

---

### Smart Contracts and Real-World Enforcement

For purely digital assets:

* Smart contracts can automatically enforce rules.

For real-world assets such as:

* Real estate
* Physical goods

Legal systems and traditional contracts may still be required to enforce ownership and obligations outside the blockchain.

Blockchain can provide transparent records, while legal frameworks provide real-world enforcement.

---

## Key Takeaways

* Blockchain enables trustless transactions.
* Bitcoin solved the double-spending problem.
* Ethereum introduced programmable smart contracts.
* Smart contracts automate agreements.
* Stablecoins maintain price stability while Bitcoin fluctuates.
* Blockchain security relies on cryptography, hashing, and consensus.
* Real-world assets often require both blockchain and legal enforcement.
